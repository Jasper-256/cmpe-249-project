        The research question, start to finish

        1. The problem

          Spacecraft docking needs accurate 6-DoF pose of the target. Direct sunlight saturates RGB cameras exactly when precision matters. Event cameras record brightness changes and do not saturate the same way, so fusing the two should help. The FRESH authors argued that learned attention fusion overfits synthetic data and used learning-free RANSAC fusion instead.

        2. The hypothesis

          A gate driven by a physically measured signal (the fraction of clipped RGB pixels per 16×16 patch) should tell the network where to trust events, and should generalize from synthetic to real better than unrestricted learned fusion. The test must separate the physical signal from simply having fewer parameters.

        3. The first surprise: synthetic data never clips

          Before any training, a screen of 384 synthetic crops found no pixel above 233 out of 255. FRESH deliberately rendered without harsh lighting, so a learned clipping gate had nothing to learn from. A stock sun-flare augmentation also failed to reach the target. The fix was a targeted specular saturation protocol (TSS) that adds glare on the spacecraft itself in half the training images, run alongside the natural protocol.

        4. Fixing the data before trusting it

          An audit found a wrong real ground truth (one sequence off by a median 59°), synthetic and real event frames encoded differently, event crops padded with a value that never occurs in synthetic data, and an evaluator that silently dropped failed pose solves. All were fixed and independently reviewed before the main experiments.

        5. Controlled comparison

          Eight fusion rules share the same pretrained HRNet encoders, decoder, data and budget; only the fusion rule changes. Matched controls include a gate with identical parameters but no clipping input, a fixed physical gate, and shuffle, zero and bypass interventions. Comparisons were pre-registered before training, with one primary question and a Holm-corrected secondary family.

        6. What the evidence says so far

          Training with glare decides robustness: every model trained on clean data collapses under glare. Fusion beats either sensor alone under glare. The explicit clipping gates do not beat plain averaging; strongly initialized gates are worst exactly on the glare frames they target. A robust pose solver improves every model about as much as the choice of fusion rule.

        7. What happens next

          When the queue finishes, every chosen checkpoint is hashed and frozen, each model is evaluated once on the 24 real sequences, and the pre-registered analysis runs under both pose solvers. A positive, mixed, null or negative answer are all reportable outcomes.

        Primary pre-registered question: on real data, does the content + clipping gate beat the content-only gate (TSS-trained), in sequence-mean rotation and translation error?
